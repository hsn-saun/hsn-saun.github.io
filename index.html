I work on human-aware autonomy for mobile robots. My research asks how a robot moving through a crowd can reason about the people around it — not as obstacles to avoid, but as agents whose intentions, interactions, and comfort shape what the robot should do next.

Concretely, this spans pedestrian trajectory forecasting, social interaction modeling, reinforcement learning for navigation policies, and the empirical study of how people actually experience a robot passing nearby. I build these as complete pipelines: sensing, human-state representation, prediction, planning, learning, control, and validation on a physical robot rather than in simulation alone.

I am advised by Prof. Yen-Chen Liu at NCKU. I came to this from mechatronics: my undergraduate thesis at Hanoi University of Industry, on an autonomous 18-DOF hexapod robot trained with deep reinforcement learning, was supervised by Assoc. Prof. Anh-Tu Nguyen, who remains a co-author on my work in mobile robot navigation. Before moving to Taiwan I led a research team at the Hanoi Intelligent Robotics Laboratory.

Research interests

  • Human-aware and social robot navigation
  • Multi-agent pedestrian trajectory prediction
  • Reinforcement learning for robot control and planning
  • Human–robot interaction and pedestrian comfort modeling
